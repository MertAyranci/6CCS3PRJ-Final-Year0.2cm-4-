\chapter{Introduction}
The widespread use of digital messaging platforms has transformed how people share personal images, maintain relationships, and negotiate boundaries in everyday communication. Despite developments in security safeguards, users continue to face substantial difficulties in controlling shared content once it reaches another person's device. Current messaging platforms provide protection against external threats but offer limited tools for managing interpersonal risks such as regret, changing relationships, or misuse of personal photos \cite{trepte_2020}. These vulnerabilities are especially concerning given that consent in digital contexts is frequently dynamic rather than static, and users may seek to modify or withdraw access to shared material over time. While recent studies in privacy, digital safety, and interpersonal communication emphasise the significance of modifiable consent, practical design solutions for major messaging apps remain underdeveloped \cite{albesher_2021, im_yes_2021}. 

\section{Motivations} 
This project focuses on WhatsApp as its primary case study because it is the most widely used messaging platform for private photo sharing, with over 3 billion monthly active users \cite{statista_whatsapp_2025}. WhatsApp employs end-to-end encryption to protect message content from external access, but this model does not extend to post-delivery control between users. Once an image is sent, the sender loses the ability to influence how it will be used, stored, or redistributed. Most existing work on consent revocation in this space focuses on legal or conceptual frameworks rather than on usable, interface-level solutions within everyday messaging platforms. This project is motivated by the need to address this gap by examining how WhatsApp can better support ongoing, revocable consent when users share photos. Although WhatsApp is the primary case study, the user needs and interaction principles explored are intended to generalise to other messaging platforms that support private photo sharing and forwarding.

\section{Scope}
This project examines pre-send and post-sharing consent revocation for photos in WhatsApp one-to-one private chats. The primary focus is on how users can modify or withdraw consent before and after sharing photos, and how interface design can support this across different social dynamics. The scope is limited to image-based content and other media types are excluded. The research is confined to user experience and interface design, investigating user-facing interaction patterns rather than underlying system architecture or encryption mechanisms. Large-scale user testing, behavioural studies, and technical feasibility assessments fall outside the project scope.

\section{Research Questions}
This project is guided by a set of research questions that aim to investigate how consent revocation can be effectively supported within WhatsApp’s messaging environment. These questions address the limitations of current functionality, the expectations and concerns of users, and the design requirements for implementing revocable consent, shapeing the direction of the thesis.

\noindent
The research questions are as follows (1 main question, 5 supporting questions):

\noindent\textbf{Main research question:} How can WhatsApp be designed to support clear, usable, and trustworthy mechanisms for revoking or modifying consent after users share photos in private conversations?
\begin{enumerate}
\item What limitations exist in WhatsApp’s current design for managing photo sharing, and how do these limitations affect users’ ability to control shared content?
\item What expectations, concerns, and behaviours do WhatsApp users have regarding post-sharing control of personal photos?
\item Which interface elements and interaction patterns best communicate photo permissions and make revocation options understandable within WhatsApp’s existing UI?
\item How can the process of revoking or modifying consent be designed to be intuitive and fair for both the sender and the recipient?
\item How do the proposed revocation features affect the’ perceived trust, safety, and sense of control of users when sharing photos on WhatsApp?
\end{enumerate}

\section{Aim and Objectives}
The aim of this project is to develop and assess user interface mechanisms that facilitate revocable, ongoing consent within WhatsApp’s photo-sharing environment, allowing users to maintain control over shared content in a transparent, intuitive, and reliable way.

\noindent
To achieve this aim, the project pursues the following objectives:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Analyze the limitations of the current WhatsApp design in relation to the control of photo-sharing.
\item Define functional and non-functional requirements for effective consent revocation features.
\item Design a set of interface components and interaction flows that demonstrate how revocable consent can be integrated into the WhatsApp user interface.
\item Develop a prototype that illustrates the full permission and revocation workflow.
\item Evaluate the usability, clarity, trustworthiness, and perceived fairness of the proposed features through user testing.
\item Reflect on the broader implications for user safety, privacy, and ethical communication design.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Contributions}
The project makes the following contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item A high-fidelity interactive prototype demonstrating a redesigned consent revocation system for photo sharing in WhatsApp-like messaging environments.
    \item A set of realistic interaction flows, UI states, and permission management behaviours modelling how revocable consent could function in practice.
    \item A usability evaluation of the proposed design, assessing clarity, perceived trustworthiness, and balance between users.
    \item A human-centred design analysis of the practical implications of enabling revocable consent for photo sharing in messaging platforms.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Key Takeaways}
The central finding of this project is that consent revocation in messaging-based photo sharing can be meaningfully supported through interface design, but only partially. The prototype evaluation showed that users valued the ability to revise permissions after sending and recognised it as an improvement over current WhatsApp controls. However, it also showed that revocation is inherently limited by screenshots, external-device capture, and prior viewing, and that users interpret permission changes as socially significant acts rather than neutral technical operations. Effective revocation design must therefore balance control, honesty about limits, and sensitivity to interpersonal dynamics.
