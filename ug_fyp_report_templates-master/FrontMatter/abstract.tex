Digital messaging platforms such as WhatsApp offer limited support for modifying or withdrawing access to shared photos. Existing features like message deletion and view-once media provide partial controls but do not support ongoing, revisable consent at the photo level. This project investigates how consent revocation can be designed as a usable interaction within WhatsApp-style photo sharing. Drawing on literature concerning dynamic consent, multi-party privacy, and privacy-focused HCI, four research gaps are identified. In response, Smart Photos is proposed which is a permission model where each shared photo carries a persistent and revisable permission state covering forwarding, downloading, view-once, time limited access, and co-subject governance. A front-end prototype was build with React, Next.js and TypeScript and evaluated through semi-structured interviews with 9 participants. Thematic reflexive analysis used and five themes are extracted which shows that participants valued revisable permissions as a meaningful improvement while recognising that revocation remains inherently partial. The findings offer design reccomendations for platform providers seeking safer photo sharing.
