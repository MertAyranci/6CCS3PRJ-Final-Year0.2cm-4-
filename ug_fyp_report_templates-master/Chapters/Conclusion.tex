\chapter{Conclusion and Future Work}
This project set out to design and evaluate interface mechanisms for
revocable consent in WhatsApp-style photo sharing. The literature review
identified four gaps around usable dynamic-consent controls,
post-delivery misunderstanding, multi-party support, and limited
WhatsApp-specific empirical evidence, and these gaps shaped the
requirements, design, and implementation of the Smart Photos prototype.

The evaluation showed that participants generally felt the model gave them more control than existing WhatsApp features.
At the same time, the project showed that consent revocation in messaging
can only ever be partial. Screenshots, prior viewing and external-device
capture remain structural limits that interface design cannot fully
prevent. This means revocation gives meaningful control over future
platform-mediated access, but does not guarantee complete erasure.
Participants who understood this were more willing to trust the feature
than those who expected full deletion. The co-subject findings also
suggest that photo-sharing consent extends beyond sender and recipient to
include people depicted in the image, though questions of shared authority
remain unresolved.

Future work should move from screenshot-based evaluation to live-prototype testing with real conversations and gender-balanced samples. It should also embed the permission model within server-side infrastructure to measure how revocation latency and offline access affect trust, as discussed in Section~\ref{sec:future-directions}.

The findings apply to WhatsApp-style messaging platforms where users exchange personal images privately and may later wish to revise the terms of that
sharing. While the prototype remains a proof-of-concept, it demonstrates that treating photo sharing as an ongoing permission process is recognised by users as a step toward more controllable sharing.
