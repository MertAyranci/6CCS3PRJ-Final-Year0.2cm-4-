\chapter{Requirements}
\section{Brief}
This chapter defines the requirements for the WhatsApp-like proof-of-concept developed in this dissertation. It translates the literature review into a scoped set of stakeholders, use cases, functional requirements, and non-functional requirements that are realistic for the present prototype. The emphasis is on sender-controlled photo permissions, post-send revocation, recipient understanding of changing access, and constrained forwarding to a secondary recipient. Broader multi-party mechanisms, such as co-subject intervention, remain important to the research problem but are not treated as core prototype requirements at this stage.

\section{Stakeholders}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Photo Sender:}
  The sender shares the photo and configures its permissions. This stakeholder requires clear pre-send and post-send controls, visibility over the current state of a shared photo, and interaction patterns that allow revocation or permission change without unnecessary confusion.

  \item \textbf{Primary Recipient:}
  The primary recipient receives the sender's photo in the original chat. This stakeholder requires clear feedback about whether a photo remains visible, whether it is limited to view-once access, and whether forwarding is allowed or blocked.

  \item \textbf{Secondary Recipient:}
  The secondary recipient appears only when the primary recipient forwards a photo. This stakeholder is necessary because the prototype examines whether sender-defined permissions continue to shape access after redistribution within the platform model.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Prototype Scope}
The current prototype is intentionally narrower than the full conceptual problem described in the literature review. It implements sender-side configuration of photo permissions, sender-side revision of those permissions after sending, recipient-side feedback about active or revoked access, view-once behaviour, and a constrained forwarding flow to a secondary recipient. It demonstrates these mechanisms at the interface and interaction level rather than as a production-grade messaging infrastructure.

The following concerns are treated as out of scope for the present requirements set and are better positioned in discussion, limitations, or future work: co-subject agency, platform-assisted removal, identity or face verification, screenshot prevention, persistent audit logging, cross-device synchronisation, and guarantees of universal deletion after disclosure.

\section{Use Cases}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{UC-S0: Sender Configures Permissions Before Sending:}
  The sender selects or uploads a photo and decides how it should be shared before it enters the chat.

  \item \textbf{UC-S1: Sender Revises Permissions After Sending:}
  The sender revisits a previously shared photo and changes its visibility, forwarding permission, or view-once setting.

  \item \textbf{UC-R1: Primary Recipient Encounters Active, Revoked, or View-Once Access:}
  The primary recipient interprets the sender's current permission state through the chat interface and acts within those limits.

  \item \textbf{UC-R2: Primary Recipient Forwards an Allowed Photo:}
  The primary recipient forwards a photo only when redistribution is permitted by the sender's current settings.

  \item \textbf{UC-SR1: Secondary Recipient Receives Constrained Forwarded Content:}
  The secondary recipient receives a forwarded photo in a separate chat view and experiences the inherited constraints defined by the original sender.
\end{enumerate}

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\subsubsection{Deferred Concerns}

The following issues remain important to the overall dissertation problem, but they are not included as core requirements for the present prototype: co-subject request workflows, co-subject conflict resolution, identity or face verification, persistent audit logging, screenshot resistance, hard guarantees against saving or offline retention, and true cross-device enforcement beyond the local proof-of-concept model. These will be considered in the Summmary and Future Work chapter.

\section{Feasible Extensions Consistent with Current Scope}
If further implementation time is available, the most suitable additions are small interface-level improvements that strengthen the current revocation story without expanding the project into a different problem domain. The strongest candidates are an optional sender explanation for permission changes, clearer confirmation feedback after revocation events, and more explicit labelling of forwarded content on the third phone. These additions would support evaluation and dissertation coherence without reintroducing the larger co-subject or infrastructure-heavy issues that are intentionally outside the current prototype scope.
